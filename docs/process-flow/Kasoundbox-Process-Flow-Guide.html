1How to read this guide

This guide shows, step by step, how the Kasoundbox system handles reservations and inventory. Each process has a flowchart (a picture of the steps) followed by an explanation in plain words. The numbers in each flowchart match the numbers in its explanation.

Who’s who

CUSTOMER
The person renting. Anyone can browse the website. An account is needed only to send a reservation request.
ADMIN
You and your dad. Only admins can confirm bookings, record payments, handle deliveries and returns, and change prices or settings.
SYSTEM
The app itself. It checks stock, computes prices, saves every change and sends notifications automatically.

The shapes

How to follow a flowchart

  • Start at the blue Start shape and follow the arrows.
  • At an amber diamond, answer the question and follow the Yes or No arrow. The small label on top says who decides: you, the system, or either.
  • A red STOP box means something must be fixed first. Its arrow shows where to go back to.
  • A dashed gray box is a side note that explains the step it points to.

3The big picture

The system has two parts that work together. The left side of the diagram is the reservation module: the customer and the booking. The right side is the inventory module: your stock.

  • Reservation module handles people and paperwork: the request, confirmation, payments, delivery and pickup.
  • Inventory module keeps count: how many of each item you own, how many are in repair, and which items are promised to which booking at which time.
  • They meet at the purple boxes: showing what’s free, holding items when a booking is confirmed, recording which units went out, and updating stock after check-in.
The golden rule

The system never lets confirmed bookings need more of an item than you have at any moment. Every step in this guide protects that rule.

4The 22-hour rule

Every rental is counted in 24-hour blocks that start at the delivery time. Each block is 22 hours with the customer plus 2 hours for pickup, checking and testing.

  • Start time = delivery time. Customers can pick any whole hour from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM. When you make a booking yourself, you can set any time.
  • Pickup is 2 hours before the start time, on the day after the last rental day.
  • Free again: the items can go to the next customer exactly at the end of the last 24-hour block, the same clock time as the start.
StartDaysPickupFree again
Oct 10, 8:00 AM1Oct 11, 6:00 AMOct 11, 8:00 AM
Oct 10, 5:00 PM1Oct 11, 3:00 PMOct 11, 5:00 PM
Oct 10, 8:00 AM3 (e.g. a wake)Oct 13, 6:00 AMOct 13, 8:00 AM
Good to know
  • Because customers start between 8:00 AM and 5:00 PM, pickups always fall between 6:00 AM and 3:00 PM.
  • Back-to-back is allowed: if booking A frees a videoke at 8:00 AM, booking B can start with it at 8:00 AM.
  • The start window and the 2-hour buffer can be changed in Settings. Changes apply to new bookings only.

A1Customer sends a reservation request

PART A
Reservation module
From a customer’s first look to a completed booking.
How a customer goes from browsing to a saved request. A request does not hold any items yet; that happens only when you confirm it (A3).

  1. Step by step

    1
    Browse the catalog. Anyone can look at items, packages and prices. No account is needed.
  2. 2
    Set the event time. The customer picks the event date, the start (delivery) time from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, and how many days.
  3. 3
    See prices and availability. For that exact time, each item shows its total price and how many are free, for example “Up to 37 available”. The quantity can’t go above that number.
  4. 4
    Add to the cart. Items and packages can be mixed, for example Party Package A plus 20 extra chairs.
  5. 5
    Sign in. Needed only when sending the request, and the cart is kept while signing in. The first time, the customer adds a full name and mobile number (09XXXXXXXXX) and accepts the privacy notice.
  6. 6
    Venue and notes. The delivery address, a landmark to help the driver, notes, and an optional promo code.
  7. 7
    Promo code check. If a code was entered, the system checks that it is active, within its dates, that the order reaches the code’s minimum amount, and that it still has uses left. If not, the customer is told why and can remove or fix it.
  8. 8
    Send the request.
  9. 9
    Final stock check. Right before saving, the system checks stock again. If a booking was confirmed in the meantime and there’s no longer enough, the customer sees “Only X left for that time” and changes the cart.
  10. 10
    Saved as REQUESTED. The request gets a code like KSB-7Q4M, and you and your dad get a notification.

At the end, the customer sees what happens next (you review it and ask for the downpayment) and is asked to allow notifications.

Good to know
  • A customer can have up to 3 open requests at a time.
  • A request must start at least 24 hours from now and at most 365 days ahead, for up to 30 days.
  • The customer can cancel their own request while it is still REQUESTED. After confirmation, only admins can cancel.
  • If the start time passes and nobody confirmed the request, it expires automatically and the customer is told.

A2Admin reviews the request

What you do when a new request comes in, before any money changes hands.

  1. Step by step

    1
    Open the request. You get a notification for every new request. Open it to see the items, the time, the venue and the customer’s details.
  2. 2
    Green or red. Each item shows green (enough stock for that time) or red (not enough). This is live: it changes if another booking gets confirmed.
  3. 3
    All green? If yes, go on to step 6.
  4. 4
    Can it be fixed? If something is red, talk to the customer by phone or Messenger. Maybe they can take fewer items, move the time, or use a different item.
  5. 5
    Edit the request. Make the change you agreed on. The colors refresh (back to step 2). If nothing works, decline the request with a short reason; the customer gets a notification.
  6. 6
    Delivery charge or discount. If needed, add a delivery charge or a manual discount (for example a suki discount). The total and the downpayment update right away.
  7. 7
    Ask for the downpayment. Tell the customer how much to pay and how. The payment instructions from Settings also appear on the customer’s reservation page.
Good to know
  • Walk-in, phone and Messenger bookings: create the booking yourself in the admin panel. It follows the same steps, and you can set any start time.
  • Refresh prices: if you changed prices after the customer sent the request, the request keeps the old prices. Press “Refresh prices” only if you want the new ones.

A3Admin confirms with the downpayment

Confirming is one button that saves the payment, checks everything, and holds the items, all at once.

  1. Step by step

    1
    Record downpayment & confirm. Open the booking and tap the button.
  2. 2
    Enter the payment. The amount, the method (GCash, bank transfer or cash) and the reference number.
  3. 3
    Paid enough? The system compares what’s paid with the required downpayment (for example 50% of the total). If it’s short, it shows how much is still needed and saves nothing.
  4. 4
    Promo still has uses left? A promo use counts only when a booking is confirmed. If the code reached its limit in the meantime, the system stops and asks you to remove the code (you’ll see the new price) or give a manual discount instead. The price never changes without you knowing.
  5. 5
    Items still available? The final stock check. If another booking took the items, the system shows exactly what’s short, for example “Only 20 Monobloc Chairs free on Oct 10; this booking needs 30”, and saves nothing. Go back to the customer to fix it (A2).
  6. 6
    Confirmed. In one go the system saves the payment, holds the items for this booking’s time, and counts the promo use. The customer gets “Booking confirmed”.
All or nothing

A confirmation either fully succeeds or saves nothing at all, not even the payment record. There is never a half-confirmed booking.

Good to know
  • Two admins, same moment: if you and your dad confirm two bookings for the last items at the same time, only one can succeed. The other gets the “what’s short” message.
  • After confirmation, only admins can change or cancel the booking, and every change checks stock again.

A5Delivery day (dispatch)

What happens on the morning of a delivery, from the run list to the customer’s door.

  1. Step by step

    1
    The run list. Every morning the dashboard lists today’s and tomorrow’s deliveries and pickups in time order. Each stop shows the time, the customer, a tap-to-call mobile number, the address and landmark, the items to load (packages are listed item by item) and the balance to collect.
  2. 2
    Load the items.
  3. 3
    Pick the units. For tracked items like videoke machines, choose the exact units you’re bringing, for example VK-01.
  4. 4
    Unit free and in service? The system only offers units that are in service and not already out with another customer. If a unit is in repair, pick another.
  5. 5
    Inclusions checklist. Tick the accessories that go with each item: mics, remote, cables. This protects you if something goes missing.
  6. 6
    Tap “Dispatch”. Everything on the booking goes out together. You can dispatch starting on the booking’s start date.
  7. 7
    Status OUT. The chosen units are marked as out, and the customer gets “Out for delivery”.
  8. 8
    Deliver and set up by the start time.
  9. 9
    Balance paid now? If the customer pays the balance at delivery, record it (amount, method, reference). If not, the balance stays on the booking to be paid later.
Print slip

You can print a delivery receipt listing the items, unit codes and inclusions, with signature lines, so there’s a record of what the customer received. It is a delivery receipt only, not a BIR official receipt.

A6Extending a rental

When a customer wants to keep the items longer, for example a wake that runs an extra day.

  1. Step by step

    1
    Extend. Open the booking (it must be OUT), tap “Extend” and enter how many days to add.
  2. 2
    Extra time OK? The system checks that no other booking needs these items during the extra time, and that the total stays within the maximum (30 days by default). If not, you can’t extend; offer a different item or keep the original pickup.
  3. 3
    New pickup and price. The pickup time moves by 24 hours per added day, and the extra days are added at the extra-day price. The balance goes up.
  4. 4
    Tell the customer the new pickup time and balance. They can also see both on their reservation page.
Example

A videoke costs ₱1,000 for the 1st day and ₱800 for each extra day. A 1-day rental extended by 2 days becomes 3 days: ₱1,000 + ₱800 × 2 = ₱2,600. The pickup moves from Oct 11, 6:00 AM to Oct 13, 6:00 AM.

A7How the price is computed

The same order every time: items first, then discounts, then extra charges. Here is a full example.

Booking KSB-7Q4M · Oct 10, 8:00 AM · 1 day
Party Package A × 1₱2,200
Extra monobloc chairs × 20 at ₱10₱200
Videoke set × 1₱1,000
Items subtotal₱3,400
Promo FIESTA10 (10%, up to ₱300)−₱300
Manual discount (suki)−₱100
Delivery charge+₱200
Total₱3,200
Downpayment needed to confirm (50%)₱1,600
Paid by GCash−₱1,600
Balance (collect on delivery)₱1,600

  1. The rules

    1
    Line price = quantity × (1st-day price + extra-day price × extra days). A blank extra-day price means every day costs the 1st-day price. Example: 50 chairs for 3 days at ₱10 for the 1st day and ₱5 for extra days = 50 × (₱10 + ₱5 + ₱5) = ₱1,000.
  2. 2
    Promo code: at most one per booking. It comes off the items subtotal, and only if the code is active, within its dates, the order reaches its minimum amount, and it has uses left.
  3. 3
    Manual discount: given by an admin (₱ or %) with a reason. It comes off after the promo.
  4. 4
    Discounts only reduce item prices. They never reduce delivery, damage, loss or late charges. To waive one of those, edit the charge itself.
  5. 5
    Rounding: percentage discounts round down to the peso (₱299.25 → ₱299). Percentage downpayments round up (₱1,600.50 → ₱1,601).
  6. 6
    Frozen prices: a booking keeps the prices from when the request was sent. Changing a price later doesn’t affect it unless you press “Refresh prices” on a pending request.
  7. 7
    Paid too much? The booking shows “Refund due ₱X”.

B1How the system checks availability

PART B
Inventory module
How the system keeps count of what you own and what is promised.
This check runs every time someone asks “can we rent this?”. It runs separately for every item in the cart.

  1. Step by step

    1
    Count usable units. Only units in service count; units in repair never do. For chairs that’s the “in service” number; for videoke it’s how many machines are marked in service.
  2. 2
    Find the bookings that overlap. Only confirmed and out bookings hold stock. Requests don’t.
  3. 3
    Find the busiest moment. Within the requested time, the system finds the moment when the most units are already in use. See the example in B2.
  4. 4
    Free = usable − busiest moment.
  5. 5
    Add up the cart. A package counts as its contents. If the cart has Package A (50 chairs) plus 20 extra chairs, the chair need is 70.
  6. 6
    Enough for every item? Yes: it can be reserved. No: the system shows “Only X left” and the busiest time, so you know what to change.
Good to know
  • Late items count as out. If items weren’t checked in after their time ended, the system treats them as still out until someone checks them in. It never assumes they came back.
  • Back-to-back is fine. A booking that frees items at 8:00 AM doesn’t overlap one that starts at 8:00 AM.
  • Repair doesn’t count, even if the item might be fixed before the event. When it’s fixed, mark it “back from repair” and it counts again.

B2Example: finding the busiest moment

You own 100 chairs. Two bookings are already confirmed, and a new request asks for 20 chairs.

  • During the new request’s time (Oct 10, 12 PM → Oct 11, 12 PM), the most chairs in use at once is 90: from Oct 10, 2 PM to Oct 11, 8 AM, when bookings A and B overlap.
  • So only 100 − 90 = 10 chairs are free for the whole period. The request for 20 can’t be confirmed, and the system says “Only 10 left”.
  • If the customer starts at Oct 11, 8:00 AM instead, booking A has ended and only B (30 chairs) is still out until 2 PM. Then 70 are free and 20 fits.
Why the busiest moment?

Chairs don’t come back in the middle of a rental. The request is only safe if there are enough chairs at every moment of its time, so the tightest moment decides.

B3Stock states

Every item is in one of a few states. Counted items are tracked as numbers; tracked units are followed one by one.

Counted items (e.g. chairs, tables)

Tracked units (e.g. videoke VK-01)

“Held” is not a place

A confirmed booking holds items for a period of time on the calendar. The items stay in service; they are just promised. That’s why confirming a booking never changes your stock numbers. Only real-life events do: repairs, losses, and new stock.

Good to know
  • For chairs, “out with the customer” isn’t a separate count. They are still yours and in service, just promised to a booking.
  • A unit can’t be retired while it is out with a customer. Check it in first.
  • A unit can also be retired straight from the shop, for example if you sell it.

B4Pickup and check-in (returns)

Check-in is your “check and test” step. It is where the inventory gets updated and extra charges are added.

  1. Step by step

    1
    Pick up. The run list shows each pickup time, for example 6:00 AM (2 hours before the next start). Bring everything back to the shop.
  2. 2
    Check-in. Open the booking and tap “Check-in”. For each item, count how many are good, damaged or missing. Test videoke units one by one and use the inclusions checklist. The counts must add up to what went out.
  3. 3
    Damaged? The damaged quantity (or unit) moves to IN REPAIR and can’t be rented until fixed. Enter a damage charge; the replacement cost is shown as a guide.
  4. 4
    Missing? The missing quantity (or unit) is written off from stock. The system adds a charge equal to the replacement cost, and you can change it.
  5. 5
    Late? If check-in happens more than 1 hour after the pickup time, the system suggests a late charge: the extra-day price for each started day late. Waive it if the delay wasn’t the customer’s fault.
  6. 6
    Released. The hold ends, and the items are free for new bookings from now.
  7. 7
    Balance ₱0? Yes: the booking is COMPLETED. No: it stays RETURNED until the customer pays; record the payment and it completes automatically.
Good to know

Everything comes back in one check-in. If a missing item turns up later, add it back with a count correction (B5) and adjust the charge.

B5Recording inventory changes

Stock is never edited directly. Every change is an action with a reason, so “why do we have 180 chairs instead of 200?” always has an answer.

ActionWhen to use itCounted item (chairs)Tracked unit (videoke)
Add stockYou bought moreIn service goes upAdd new units with codes
Send to repairSomething broke at the shopIn service ↓, in repair ↑Unit → in repair
Back from repairIt’s fixedIn repair ↓, in service ↑Unit → in service
Write offLost or broken for goodIn service (or in repair) ↓Unit → retired (not while out)
Count correctionA physical count doesn’t matchIn service set to the counted number—

  1. Step by step

    1
    Pick the action in Inventory.
  2. 2
    Enter the details: the quantity (or the unit code) and the reason. A reason is required for write-offs and corrections.
  3. 3
    Saved and logged: who did it, when and why.
  4. 4
    Re-check: the system checks every upcoming confirmed booking against the new stock.
  5. 5
    Still enough? If not, a SHORT alert shows the item, the time, how many are missing, and which bookings are affected. Fix it by repairing faster, swapping units, renting from a partner, or calling the customer to adjust.
The change is always saved

It already happened in real life, so the system records it even if it causes a shortage. The SHORT alert makes sure you see the effect on upcoming bookings in time.
